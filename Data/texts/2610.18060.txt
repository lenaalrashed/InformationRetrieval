Abstract
Task-oriented dialogue understanding remains
underexplored for Bangla, particularly in mul-
tilingual settings involving English and ro-
manized Bangla (Banglish).
We introduce
BanglaTOD, a parallel tri-lingual benchmark
comprising 209 multi-turn dialogues across
nine Bangladesh-speciﬁc service domains and
64 intents. Each conversational turn is aligned
across Bangla, English, and Banglish, yield-
ing 4,689 language-speciﬁc turn records anno-
tated with intents, token-level slots, state up-
dates, and accumulated dialogue states. We es-
tablish context-aware baselines for joint intent
classiﬁcation and slot ﬁlling using mBERT,
BERT-base-uncased, and BanglaBERT. Cross-
language analysis reveals substantial perfor-
mance differences across models and linguis-
tic representations, while removing dialogue
history reduces intent accuracy by 19.0–25.7
percentage points.
BanglaTOD provides a
foundation for multilingual dialogue under-
standing and future dialogue state tracking re-
search in Bangladesh-speciﬁc conversational
applications. The dataset is publicly available
at Hugging Face.
1
Introduction
Task-oriented dialogue systems rely on identi-
fying user intents, extracting relevant slot val-
ues, and maintaining conversational context across
turns (Budzianowski et al., 2018; Chen et al.,
2019; Zhu et al., 2020; Rastogi et al., 2020). De-
spite advances in multilingual natural language
understanding (Schuster et al., 2019; Ding et al.,
2022), resources for Bangla task-oriented dialogue
remain limited (Khan et al., 2021; Karim and
Uzuner, 2025; Sadeq et al., 2020).
This limita-
tion is particularly relevant in Bangladesh, where
digital communication frequently involves Bangla,
English, and Banglish (Alam et al., 2025; Hos-
sain et al., 2026; Bari et al., 2026). Variations in

language, script, and spelling introduce additional
challenges for intent recognition, slot extraction,
and dialogue state tracking.
Existing multilingual resources, including Mul-
tiATIS++ (Xu et al., 2020), MTOP (Li et al.,
2021), and GlobalWoZ (Ding et al., 2022), have
advanced cross-lingual task-oriented NLU but pro-
vide limited coverage of Bangladesh-speciﬁc con-
versational settings. Bangla NLP resources have
primarily addressed language modeling, general
language understanding, and generation (Bhat-
tacharjee et al., 2022, 2023).
More recently,
Bangla intent classiﬁcation and slot ﬁlling have
been investigated using translated task-oriented
datasets (Karim and Uzuner, 2025). However, re-
sources containing multi-turn conversations with
parallel Bangla, English, and Banglish utterances
and structured dialogue-state annotations remain
scarce. Translated corpora also do not contain re-
alistic ontology for Bangladesh.
To address this gap, we introduce BanglaTOD,
a parallel tri-lingual benchmark for task-oriented
dialogue understanding in Bangladesh.
The
dataset comprises 209 manually constructed multi-
turn dialogues spanning nine service domains and
64 intents.
Each conversational turn is aligned
across Bangla, English, and Banglish, producing
4,689 language-speciﬁc turn records. The annota-
tions include intents, token-level slots, turn-level
state updates, and accumulated dialogue states,
supporting both utterance-level understanding and
future dialogue state tracking research.
We ad-
ditionally establish context-aware baselines using
three pretrained encoders and examine their per-
formance across linguistic representations.

Dataset and Methodology
2.1
Dataset Coverage and Construction
BanglaTOD comprises 209 multi-turn dialogues
across nine Bangladesh-speciﬁc service domains
and 64 intents. Domains were selected for their rel-
evance to everyday service interactions, informed
by existing task-oriented dialogue resources. Four
annotators constructed the dialogues through pair-
wise role-playing (user and system), to simulate
realistic service interactions.
Each intent repre-
sents a distinct user goal within its domain. All di-
alogues and annotations were manually reviewed
to identify and correct errors. Table 1 summarizes
the dataset coverage.
Dialogues were constructed as user–system in-
teractions spanning 2–10 turns.
They include
follow-up requests that refer to information intro-
duced in earlier turns, capturing contextual depen-
dencies beyond isolated utterances.

Experimental Setup
Data and Split.
We partition the 209 dialogues
into training (137), validation (36), and test (36)
sets using dialogue-level split with intent-based
stratiﬁcation. All three parallel representations of

each dialogue remain in the same partition to pre-
vent cross-language leakage.
Models
and
Training.
We
ﬁne-tune
three
encoders:
bert-base-multilingual-cased
(mBERT)
(Devlin
et
al.,
2019),
BERT-
base-uncased
(BERT-base)
(Devlin
et
al.,
2019),
and
sagorsarker/bangla-bert-base
(BanglaBERT) (Sarker, 2020).
Each encoder
is trained on pooled Bangla, English, and Ban-
glish data and evaluated separately on each
language. Following Chen et al. (2019), the joint
architecture uses a two-layer intent classiﬁer
(768→256→64) and a linear BIO slot classiﬁer.
Domain predictions are derived from the predicted
intents. Models are trained using AdamW with a
weighted joint loss (Lint + 1.5Lslot) across three
seeds (42, 43, 44). Additional training details are
provided in Appendix A. Each target utterance is
encoded with up to three preceding turns from the
same dialogue and language, separated by [SEP]
tokens (Padmakumar et al., 2023).
Evaluation.
We report intent accuracy (equiv-
alent to micro-F1 for single-label classiﬁcation),
macro-F1, and weighted-F1. Slot ﬁlling is eval-
uated using entity-level precision, recall, and F1
based on exact span and type matches. We ad-
ditionally report domain accuracy and joint exact
match (EM), which requires both a correct intent
and an exact match of the complete slot-label se-
quence. Results are averaged across three seeds.
4
Results and Discussion
4.1
Cross-Language Analysis
Table 4 shows substantial differences across the
three language representations, despite their par-
allel conversational content.
mBERT achieves
the strongest overall performance and remains
relatively consistent across languages, with in-
tent accuracy ranging from 0.573 to 0.625.
In
contrast, BERT-base performs substantially better
on English and Banglish than on Bangla, while
BanglaBERT achieves its highest intent accuracy
on Bangla (0.659) but transfers poorly to the other
representations.
These patterns are consistent with differences
in pretraining language and script coverage (Rust
et al., 2021; Wu and Dredze, 2020).
The par-
allel structure of BanglaTOD enables such com-
parisons while controlling for conversational con-
tent, revealing the challenges of supporting native-
script, English, and romanized language represen-
tations within a shared dialogue system.
4.2
Fine-Grained Intent and Slot Challenges
Across all backbones, domain classiﬁcation is con-
siderably more accurate than intent classiﬁcation.
For example, mBERT achieves 0.859 domain ac-
curacy but only 0.601 intent accuracy.
Intent
macro-F1 is also consistently lower than weighted-
F1 (0.408 versus 0.623 for mBERT), suggesting
difﬁculties with infrequent intent categories.
Error
inspection
further
reveals
confusion
between semantically related intents, including
cash_out_information
and
transaction_support,
and
between
tour_cancellation
and
mod-
ify_booking.
Government-service
intents,
which constitute the largest intent inventory in the
dataset, are particularly challenging. These obser-
vations highlight the difﬁculty of distinguishing
ﬁne-grained user goals in a limited-data setting.
Slot ﬁlling presents an additional challenge.
Even the strongest model achieves only 0.330
entity-level F1. Errors frequently involve omitted
slots and incomplete multi-word spans, whereas
short, regularly formatted values are recovered
more reliably. Together, these results suggest that
ﬁne-grained intent recognition and structured slot
extraction remain important challenges for low-
resource task-oriented dialogue understanding.
4.3
Effect of Dialogue Context
To assess the importance of conversational history,
we retrain each backbone without preceding turns.
Table 5 summarizes the ablation. Removing con-
text reduces intent accuracy by 19.0–25.7 percent-
age points across the three backbones. The cor-
responding reductions in token-level slot macro-
F1 are smaller.
This pattern suggests that pre-
ceding turns are particularly useful for resolving
context-dependent user intentions, such as follow-
up questions that omit previously established in-
formation.
The consistent improvements across
backbones demonstrate the importance of multi-
turn context and motivate evaluation beyond iso-
lated utterances.

5
Conclusion
We introduce BanglaTOD, a parallel Bangla, En-
glish, and Banglish benchmark for multi-turn task-
oriented dialogue understanding in Bangladesh-
speciﬁc service domains.
Baseline evaluations
reveal substantial variation across language rep-
resentations, persistent challenges in ﬁne-grained
intent classiﬁcation and slot extraction,
and
clear beneﬁts from incorporating dialogue his-
tory.
These ﬁndings establish BanglaTOD as a
resource for studying multilingual and context-
dependent dialogue understanding in low-resource
settings.
Future work includes hierarchical in-
tent modelling, Banglish normalization, and end-
to-end dialogue state tracking.

Limitations
BanglaTOD is a relatively small, manually con-
structed dataset with uneven coverage across do-
mains, intents, and slot types. Role-played conver-
sations may not fully capture the linguistic diver-
sity, informal expressions, and spelling variations
encountered in real-world interactions, particu-
larly in Banglish. Although the parallel represen-
tations enable controlled comparisons, they do not
fully reﬂect naturally occurring code-switching or
language-speciﬁc conversational patterns.
