ABSTRACT
Text summarization is an important task for advancing natural language processing (NLP) research
and applications in Indic languages. Despite progress in extractive and abstractive summarization,
monolingual summarization (MS) and cross-lingual summarization (CLS) remain relatively under-
explored for these languages. Recent advances in Transformer architectures [1], increased dataset
availability, and stronger pretrained language models (PLMs) offer new opportunities to address this
gap. In this work, we construct cross-lingual training data from the monolingual Marathi MahaSum
corpus [2] and combine it with the substantially smaller cross-lingual PMIndiaSum dataset [3] to
fine-tune IndicTrans2 [4], an Indic neural machine translation (NMT) model. We evaluate the re-
sulting models using ROUGE, chrF, and chrF++ metrics, focusing primarily on Marathi-to-English
CLS due to computational constraints. We also discuss the applicability of our approach to the
English-to-Marathi direction. Our experiments show that fine-tuning a pretrained translation model
for CLS substantially outperforms the IndicBART [5]- and mBART-50 [6]-based baselines reported
in previous work on CLS.
Keywords Indic LLM · Text Summarization · Cross Lingual Summarization
1
Introduction
The capabilities of large language models for text-processing tasks have improved substantially over the past decade.
The introduction of Transformer-based [1] deep-learning models was a major catalyst. Summarization remains one of
the most challenging text-generation tasks and is commonly divided into two categories:
Extractive This approach selects sentences from the source document that convey its most important information.
Abstractive This approach generates new text that conveys the source document’s key ideas concisely.
Text summarization for English and many European languages has matured to the point of broad product deployment. In
contrast, comparatively limited work has addressed monolingual and cross-lingual summarization for Indic languages.
Their morphological richness, linguistic diversity, and limited availability of large high-quality datasets make the task
challenging. This paper investigates abstractive cross-lingual summarization for the Marathi–English language pair
with focus on Marathi-English direction. It uses a novel approach in doing CLS. The results vary based on corpus
availability and various other factors.

Related Work
Before highly capable Indic language models became available, summarization research for Indic languages was
constrained by the limited availability of large, high-quality datasets. Apart from the size of the dataset, various other
criteria help determine the quality of summarization:
• Conciseness in the generated summary

• Completeness in capturing the essence of text
• Quality of language in generated summary

Zhu et al. [7] proposed neural cross-lingual summarization for English–Chinese and Chinese–English. They use
round-trip translation to construct a CLS dataset from a monolingual summarization corpus and found that neural CLS
outperformed conventional pipeline approaches.
An early study by Sarkar [8] uses an extractive approach for Bengali. It focuses on monolingual summarization. HindiS-
umm [9] is an abstractive Hindi summarization corpus comprising 570,000 article–summary pairs from Navbharat
Times across 21 domains. Authors claim that the dataset has been evaluated against above criteria by human experts.
They fine tuned mT5 [10] and mBART25 [11] pre-trained models on this dataset and evaluate the ensemble performance
on this and various other summarization datasets. They claim that HindiSumm demonstrates better performance with
various datasets, based on ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2 and ROUGE-N [12] scores.
ILSUM 2.0 [13] was created for the ILSUM shared task. It supports 3 Indian languages - Hindi, Gujarati and Bengali.
For each language approximately 15,000 new-article summary pairs are included. One of the tasks is monolingual
summarization. The Hindi dataset contains 24,225 entries with mean summary length of 39.38 and mean compression
ratio of 14.78. Like Ulrana, IndicBART and mBART-50 [6] PLMs were fine-tuned and evaluated using the ROUGE and
BERTScore [14] metrics.
MahaSum [2] is an Marathi summarization dataset containing approximately 25,000 news articles paired with manually
vetted summaries. Authors fine tuned IndicBART based PLM and claim to that it is a gold-standard abstractive
summarization dataset for Marathi. They claim significantly better performance better performance against XL-
Sum [15] using ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2 and ROUGE-L metrics.
Work by Jothir Adithya T. K. et al. [16] for Marathi, focuses on MS. More recent studies have investigated cross-lingual
abstractive summarization for Indic languages. Deshpande et al. [17] proposed two CLS pipelines: translating the
source text before summarization, and summarizing in the source language before translating the summary. They also
proposed an evaluation metric that combines concept coverage, semantic similarity, and relevance, and validated it
using MuRIL [18].
CrossSum [19] is a cross-lingual dataset that covers 1500+ language pairs and has around 1.7 million entries. They fine
tuned mT5 [10] base model using m2o (many-to-many), m2m (many-to-many) and o2m (one-to-many) approaches to
evaluate the performance of this dataset. They claim that the MLS (Multistage Language Sampling) algorithm using
m2m approach consistently outperforms m2o and o2m models. Their evaluation metrics are ROUGE-2 and their own
LaSE (Language-agnostic Summary Evaluation). Their base models are mT5 and M2M-100 [20].
Urlana’s work [21] comprehensively analyses MS and CLS datasets with respect to summary quality, relevance,
readability, and creativity. He also introduces PMIndiaSum [3] - a high-quality multilingual and cross-lingual headline-
summarization dataset covering 14 Indic languages including Marathi. He fine tunes IndicBART [5] and mBART-50-
large [6] models to evaluate the performance of this dataset. He also uses ROUGE-2 and ROUGE-L metrics.
After perusing this work, we can clearly see that the choice of pretrained language model (PLM) strongly influences the
quality of MS and CLS. A model requires robust monolingual/multilingual understanding for MS/CLS in order to adapt
effectively with limited fine-tuning data. Dataset quality is also quite important. Following section analyzes various
datasets relevant to our work.
3
Dataset Selection & Preparation
Fine-tuning pretrained language models (PLMs) for cross-lingual summarization requires high-quality document–
summary datasets. For Marathi, available cross-lingual resources are limited in size and vary in construction quality. We
therefore consider four publicly available resources: MahaSum [2], CrossSum [19], XL-Sum [15], and PMIndiaSum [3].
These datasets differ in language coverage, source domain, corpus size, and document–summary construction method,
providing complementary settings for studying Marathi cross-lingual summarization. MahaSum and the Marathi
portion of XL-Sum provide Marathi monolingual article–summary pairs from which synthetic cross-lingual training
examples are constructed. The English portion of XL-Sum is also used to construct additional synthetic examples where
appropriate. PMIndiaSum and CrossSum provide cross-lingual evaluation data involving English and Marathi, although
they differ in source, scale, and data-collection methodology.
We report Cross-lingual summarization results primarily for Marathi-to-English (MR →EN) direction. In the MR →
EN direction, the input document is in Marathi and the target summary is in English.

MahaSum is the primary source of synthetic cross-lingual training data. For MR →EN dataset, we retain the original
Marathi documents and translate the Marathi summaries into English. This produces Marathi-document/English-
summary pairs while preserving the human-produced Marathi summaries. For EN →MR dataset, we retain the original
Marathi summaries and translate the documents into English. Thus, in both directions, one component of each original
Marathi article–summary pair is machine-translated, while the other component is retained. As a secondary source of
synthetic cross-lingual dataset, we use XL-Sum corpora.
We use a neural machine translation (NMT) approach for both translation operations. Machine translation can introduce
noise relative to native cross-lingual data, we evaluate the translation pipeline separately using round-trip translation.
We use IndicTrans2-200M [22] for translation. For longer-context translation when documents in Marathi are translated
to English, we use the Rotary IndicTrans2 model, which incorporates rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) [23] and
supports longer input sequences [4]. The translation models also handle code-mixed content appearing in the MahaSum
and XL-Sum corpora.

MahaSum
MahaSum [2] is a Marathi news-summarization corpus developed to address the limited availability of
resources for abstractive Marathi summarization. The corpus contains 25,374 articles collected from Lokmat and
Loksatta newspapers; each instance includes a headline, a summary, and the corresponding article text. The summaries
were manually verified during the corpus construction.
In our work, MahaSum is the primary source of synthetic CLS training data. For English-to-Marathi CLS, we retain
the original Marathi summaries and translate the article texts into English. This produces English-document/Marathi-
summary pairs while preserving the human-produced Marathi summaries. For Marathi-to-English direction, we retain
the original Marathi article texts and translate the summaries into English. For the translation in both directions, we
use an NMT-based approach. NMT can introduce more noise compared to native cross-lingual dataset. For NMT,
we use IndicTrans2-200M [22] model. prajdabre/rotary-indictrans2-indic-en-dist-200M [4] is an improved version of
original IndicTrans2-200M model as it supports longer context as it uses Rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) [23].
The translation models can process code-mixed content appearing in the MahaSum and XL-Sum corpora.
Translation-fidelity validation
We assess the fidelity of the summary-translation pipeline through round-trip transla-
tion on all 1,667 examples in the MahaSum validation split. Each original Marathi summary was translated into English
and then back-translated into Marathi. For back-translation, we use prajdabre/rotary-indictrans2-en-indic-dist-200M
model. We compared the resulting Marathi summary with the original reference using chrF [24] and chrF++ [25]
metrics. As shown in Table 1, the pipeline achieved mean chrF and chrF++ scores of 56.34 and 49.97, respectively. The
mean character- and word-length ratios were 0.967 and 0.994, indicating that summary length was closely preserved.
These results indicate moderate round-trip lexical fidelity and strong length preservation; they validate the translation
pipeline used to construct the synthetic data, but do not replace direct evaluation of the final CLS models.
We also assess the fidelity for document/text-translation pipeline using the round-trip translation approach as described
above. As shown in Table 2, the pipeline achieved mean chrF and chrF++ scores of 63.61 and 59.15, respectively. The
mean character- and word-length ratios were 0.971 and 0.994, indicating that text length was closely metrics.
Metrics for document translation are marginally better than those obtained for much shorter summary back-translation.
In both the cases, we manually checked random samples from the synthetic datasets generated in both the cases and
found the translation quality to be satisfactory.
We didn’t evaluate the translation quality for the synthetic XL-Sum datasets, as the process is computationally expensive.
However, given the above results for MahaSum, the quality is expected to be good. This is further corroborated by the
results obtained after fine tuning PLMs with these datasets.
CrossSum
CrossSum is a multilingual news-summarization resource constructed from BBC content and covering 45
languages and 1500+ language pairs. The complete collection contains approximately 1.7 million article–summary
pairs, including 1,181 Marathi examples [19]. We use the Marathi subset as an additional reference corpus alongside
PMIndiaSum and to compare model behaviour across Marathi-English and English-Marathi summarization resources.
Its the smallest of CLS datasets we use in our work. As noted by the authors, dataset is not curated by humans.

XL-Sum
XL-Sum is a multilingual news-summarization resource constructed from BBC content and covering 44
languages. We use the Marathi corpus as additional Marathi-English summarization resource by translating summary
field to English. Similarly, we use the English subset which which is orders of magnitude larger as an additional

behaviour across Marathi-English and English-Marathi summarization resources. Like MahaSum, we use translation
approach to construct synthetic CLS data-set from XL-Sum-MR and XL-Sum-EN datasets.
PMIndiaSum
PMIndiaSum is a multilingual and cross-lingual summarization resource for Indian languages. It
support 14 languages and 196 language directions. The dataset provides both monolingual and cross-lingual document–
headline pairs [3]. It contains approximately 4,400 examples. All texts have been translated by humans as they belong
to the official press releases by India PM office. We use this as primary reference in our work. Its is too small to be
useful as the only dataset. As a result, we use it in combination with XL-Sum and MahaSum. We split it in 3 parts
- train, validation and test split. The test split is never seen during fine-tuning. It is used to evaluate the quality of
fine-tuned models.
4
Summarization Approach and PLM Selection
Our goal is to achieve high quality cross-lingual summarization for Marathi. We considered several PLMs for fine-tuning.
The principal criterion was strong coverage of Marathi, other Indic languages, and English. Although BART-based
models [26] are effective for abstractive MS, adapting them to CLS can be challenging. We therefore investigate a novel
approach based on IndicTrans2 [22] PLM which supports all 22 scheduled Indian languages. We investigate the use
of IndicTrans2 as the primary generative backbone for Marathi–English cross-lingual summarization. IndicTrans2 is
known to have excellent cross-lingual understanding and performs at par with or better than most other Open Source
models NLLB [27] and M2M-100 [20], and commercial ones from Microsoft and Google. It performs well in our
experiments for both English-to-Marathi and Marathi-to-English CLS.
We use FlashAttention-2 [28] during fine-tuning to improve training speed and reduce memory consumption. The
primary models examined for Marathi-English CLS were:
1. indictrans2-indic-en-1B
2. rotary-indictrans2-indic-en-1B
3. indictrans2-indic-en-dist-200M
4. rotary-indictrans2-indic-en-dist-200M
We also study Rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) [23] based models as they enable contexts of up to 2,048 tokens.
For English-Marathi direction, corresponding en-indic models were used. In contrast to the work of Urlana et al. [3],
we do not use IndicBART [5] or mT5 [10].

Experimental Setup
Our training pipeline uses the Hugging Face Trainer API. We fine-tune the PLMs on NVIDIA A100 GPUs with
40GB VRAM1. The pipeline uses torchrun, a PyTorch [29] utility, to distribute fine-tuning across multiple GPUs.
For repeatability, accuracy and reproducibility, we used MLflow [30] to log training parameters, track metrics, and
preserve artifacts for each run. Checkpointing was enabled to enable resuming the training run after interruption, and
the best-performing checkpoint from each run was retained.
The experiments are organized by the two directions defined in Table 4. All reported results are therefore identified
explicitly as either Marathi-to-English. However we have reported the numbers for Marathi-to-English direction
as this direction is more challenging and significant GPU resources are needed to obtain identical number in the
English-Marathi direction.

Evaluation and Validation
ROUGE [12] is a widely used metric for text summarization, and BERTScore [14] is commonly used to evaluate
generated text semantically. We use ROUGE-L to select the best-performing checkpoint from each training run. We
also manually inspect generated summaries for meaningfulness, coherence, and coverage of the source document’s
central content.

Results
We first establish the baseline performance on the PMIndia-MR test set using IndicBART [5] and mBART-50 [6]
models. The numbers are not readily available elsewhere in published literature. We fine-tune the baseline models with
same inputs as our IndicTrans2 models. For the baseline, ROUGE and BERTScore-F1 metrics are reported in Table 6
and Table 7 report the baseline numbers. Best results are obtain across all the metrics for baseline model fine-tuned with
PMIndiaSum+MahaSum dataset. It also achieves highest chrF and chrF++ scores at 31.8064 & 30.2792, respectively.
Figure 2 shows training and validation metric trajectories for indictrans2-indic-en-dist-200M fine-tuning.
Table 8 presents a training-data ablation for MR→EN cross-lingual summarization. All models were initialized from
the indictrans2-indic-en-dist-200M checkpoint and evaluated on the same held-out PMIndiaSum MR→EN test
set. The model trained on the combined PMIndiaSum and MahaSum datasets achieved the strongest performance, with
ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L scores of 75.65, 56.37, and 72.58, respectively, as well as a BERTScore-F1 of
99.15. Compared with PMIndiaSum-only training, the combined-data configuration improved ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2,
and ROUGE-L by 3.11, 4.02, and 3.17 points, respectively.
Adding XL-Sum Marathi to PMIndiaSum resulted in smaller improvements, increasing ROUGE-1 from 72.55 to
72.99, ROUGE-2 from 52.36 to 53.72, and ROUGE-L from 69.41 to 70.35. In contrast, models trained exclusively on
MahaSum or XL-Sum Marathi performed substantially worse than the models trained on PMIndiaSum. The MahaSum-
only configuration obtained ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L scores of 40.96, 23.23, and 37.42, respectively,
whereas the XL-Sum-Marathi-only configuration obtained scores of 40.35, 23.64, and 37.22. These differences may
reflect variation in dataset size, domain, summary style, preprocessing, or the alignment between each dataset and the
PMIndiaSum evaluation distribution.

All models were evaluated on the same held-out PMIndiaSum MR→EN test set. The model trained on the combined
PMIndiaSum and MahaSum datasets achieved the strongest ROUGE performance, obtaining ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2,
and ROUGE-L scores of 72.50, 53.31, and 69.81, respectively. Compared with PMIndiaSum-only training, this

achieved the highest chrF and chrF++ scores, at 64.99 and 62.57, respectively.
Adding XL-Sum Marathi to PMIndiaSum produced a mixed effect: ROUGE-1 increased from 72.02 to 72.25, whereas
ROUGE-2 decreased from 53.20 to 52.72 and ROUGE-L decreased from 69.71 to 69.45. Models trained exclusively
on MahaSum or XL-Sum Marathi performed substantially worse than those trained with PMIndiaSum. These results
suggest that MahaSum provides limited but measurable complementary information when combined with PMIndiaSum,
whereas XL-Sum Marathi does not consistently improve performance under the present experimental setup.
Based on these results, we found that best performing model (SOTA) for Marathi-English CLS is the IndicTrans2-200M
PLM fine tuned on PMIndiaSum+MahaSum dataset. Our approach improves upon the existing baseline significantly
using the most important datasets. Why smaller 200M models performs better than 1B model needs further investigation.

Conclusion
Cross-lingual summarization remains challenging for Indian languages. Continued advances in pretrained language
models and the availability of larger, higher-quality datasets should improve performance. Future work should investigate
stronger robust human evaluation, and methods for improving factual consistency and cross-lingual faithfulness.
Lowering inference cost is also important as it makes multi-lingual content much more accessible.
9
Future Work
We plan to explore how much multilingual capacity is actually necessary for cross-lingual summarization. Exploring
Deep Compression [31] approaches to reduce the inference cost of Indic NLP models is the logical next step. IndicTrans2
shares decoder input/output embeddings, and the decoder output projection represents a substantial portion of the
parameter count. Our goal is to focus on English-Marathi language pair. We hope that Quantization and Language Pair
specialization can yield substantial model compression.
