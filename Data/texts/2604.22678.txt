Department of Engineering

Abstract
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is
used in Knowledge-based Visual Question An-
swering (KB-VQA) to ground answers in ex-
ternal knowledge. This is commonly done by
collecting multiple retrieved documents into
a single context for answer generation. This
can have drawbacks. Concatenation may not
scale well to deep retrieval lists due to input
context length constraints. There may be “lost-
in-the-middle” effects, and the contributions
of individual documents to answer generation
can be unclear. We propose Bayesian Ensem-
ble Retrieval-Augmented Generation (BERAG)
and Bayesian Ensemble Fine-Tuning (BEFT)
as a RAG framework in which language models
are conditioned on individual retrieved docu-
ments rather than a single combined context.
BERAG computes document posteriors as en-
semble weights that are updated token by to-
ken using Bayes’ rule during answer genera-
tion. BERAG easily handles deep retrieval lists
through its parallelizing document processing.
As a position-invariant ensemble method, BE-
RAG avoids “lost-in-the-middle” effects. BE-
RAG document posteriors are strong indicators
of individual document relevance. We incor-
porate these posteriors into a Top-P inference
pruning procedure, implemented in vLLM. Rel-
ative to concatenative RAG, Top-P BERAG
offers reduced latency, faster inference, and
strong VQA performance on KB-VQA bench-
marks, E-VQA and Infoseek, and long-context
VQA tasks like SlideVQA and Multi-modal
Needle-in-a-Haystack.

Introduction
Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) is widely
adopted to tackle Knowledge-based Visual Ques-
tion Answering (KB-VQA). Grounding the answer
generator, often a Vision Language Model (VLM),
in retrieved documents that contain relevant knowl-
edge is vital for KB-VQA question answering.
Prior works often perform this grounding by con-
catenating all retrieved documents to form a single
context, appending this context to the multi-modal
query, and passing the long context to VLM for an-
swer generation. While simple, this concatenative
grounding approach has several limitations: (1) it
is challenging to use deep retrieval lists of lengthy
documents (e.g., Top-50 retrieved documents) for
question answering due to maximal context length
constraints and degraded performance when han-
dling long context. A well-studied detrimental ef-
fect is known as “lost-in-the-middle” (Liu et al.,
2024), where models neglect relevant information
present in the middle of their context. Recent stud-
ies find that VLMs exhibit heightened sensitivity
to positional arrangements compared to uni-modal
settings (Yao et al., 2025). (2) It is difficult to
determine how individual retrieved documents con-
tribute to answer generation, in that the lack of
document attribution poses challenges in verify-
ing responses in practical deployment with human
users.
To tackle these challenges, we revisit an ensem-
ble approach to document grounding that was pro-
posed in early RAG work (Lewis et al., 2020):
“the top K documents are retrieved using the re-
triever, and the generator produces the output se-
quence probability for each document, which are
then marginalized”. We extend this approach by
incorporating a Bayesian posterior probability over
each document in response generation and propos-
ing a training procedure that directly optimizes
for this form of RAG generation. We term our
inference procedure Bayesian Ensemble Retrieval-
Augmented Generation (BERAG)1 and our end-to-
end training procedure Bayesian Ensemble Fine-
Tuning (BEFT). We show this formalism eliminates
“lost-in-the-middle” effects as all documents are
treated as equal in the ensemble and also enables

the use of much deeper retrieval lists under prac-
tical VRAM budgets. By modeling the document
posterior, BERAG also explicitly quantifies docu-
ment contribution, which we show can be useful for
predicting whether retrieval is successful as well as
for accelerating decoding.
Our experiments on E-VQA (Mensink et al.,
2023) and Infoseek (Chen et al., 2023) show mod-
els trained with BEFT to generate answers follow-
ing BERAG give strong performance compared to
state-of-the-art systems. We show that this gain de-
rives from the ability to utilize the higher recall rate
for deep retrieval whereas prior systems using con-
catenative grounding see degradation when given
a long retrieval list. To our knowledge, these are
the first systematic results on improving KB-VQA
via utilizing very deep retrieval lists. We further
analyze the inference performance of BERAG sys-
tems and propose an acceleration technique based
on the posterior distribution. With this, BERAG is
substantially faster than concatenative RAG both
in prefilling and in decoding. To summarize:
• We propose BERAG and BEFT, an infer-
ence and end-to-end training framework for
ensemble-based RAG (Sec.3).
• We show strong performance on E-VQA and
Infoseek (Sec.4.2, 4.3) compared to recent sys-
tems. BERAG with our Top-P pruning is also
faster than concatenative RAG in inference
(Sec. 4.5).
• We show BERAG resolves the “lost-in-the-
middle” effect via position-invariant ensem-
bling on KB-VQA tasks (Sec.4.4).
• As additional results, we show (1) BEFT
learns to predict whether retrieval is suc-
cessful; (2) BERAG performs well on long-
context VQA tasks like SlideVQA and MM-
Needle; and (3) the document posterior en-
ables more fine-grained error attribution and
thereby enhances interpretability. These are
reported in the appendices.
These establish BERAG as a compelling alterna-
tive to the usual concatenative RAG for KB-VQA.

Related Work
Knowledge-based Visual Question Answering
(KBVQA)
In KBVQA, the system is tasked to
answer an image-related question based on knowl-
edge in retrieved documents. E-VQA (Mensink
et al., 2023) and Infoseek (Chen et al., 2023)
are widely used Wikipedia-based KBVQA bench-
marks, covering knowledge about animals and
plants, as well as facts on sites and entities. Re-
cent progress has been driven primarily by stronger
retrieval: PreFLMR (Lin et al., 2023) shows that
fine-grained late interaction substantially improves
document retrieval for KBVQA, EchoSight (Yan
and Xie, 2024) introduces a dedicated mult-modal
reranker to improve retrieval, and ReflectiVA (Coc-
chi et al., 2025) adds reflective tokens to the model
for deciding whether to retrieve and evaluating pas-
sage relevance.
Joint optimisation of retrieval and answer gen-
eration in RAG
Previous works focus on joint
retriever-answer generator training (Long et al.,
2025; Zhang et al., 2022; Lin and Byrne, 2022).
Most relevant to our work is RetGen (Zhang et al.,
2022), which introduces Actor-Critic style joint
training objective to include retriever score by con-
sidering the marginalization in Eq.2. In contrast
to their focus on retriever-generator joint optimiza-
tion, we train a single probabilistic answer gener-
ation system. This formalism yields supervised
fine-tuning objectives with token-level supervision
signals for document weighting, whereas RetGen
provides a sequence-level correction signal for the
retriever. We discuss other related work in Ap-
pendix B.

BEFT Gradient
We find empirically that BEFT
learns a strong prior distribution.
This can be
explained by the gradient of the BEFT loss. It
can be shown that the next-token likelihood mod-
ulates how BEFT maximizes the document poste-
rior. If token yj is more likely under document z1
than under document z2, BEFT allocates a larger
gradient magnitude toward maximizing the poste-
rior PΘ(z1 | y<j, x, Z) than toward maximizing
PΘ(z2 | y<j, x, Z).
Because the document posterior is related to the
document prior through Bayes’ rule, it can further
be shown that the gradient with respect to the prior
probabilities is proportional to the posterior proba-
bilities. This suggests that BEFT can be interpreted
as a form of self-supervision that learns evidence
selection directly from question-answering training.

Experiments on KB-VQA
4.1
Experimental Setup
Datasets
We experiment on Encyclopedic-VQA
(E-VQA)
(Mensink et al., 2023) and Infos-
eek (Chen et al., 2023). For training, we use the
train-test splits and passage sets as compiled in the
Multi-Modal Knowledge Retrieval (M2KR) Bench-
mark (Lin et al., 2024). For inference, we report
the original and the M2KR versions of E-VQA to
compare against prior works. The M2KR version
has 150K passages whereas the original version
has 2M passages. For Infoseek, we report on the
M2KR test split which contains 4.7K examples
derived from the original full validation set, and ad-
ditionally report BEFT results on the full validation
set of 71,335 examples.
Evaluation
We use Recall@K to evaluate re-
trieval based on the official ground-truth document
labels in the E-VQA and Infoseek datasets, not
Pseudo-Recall. For VQA performance, we use the
official VQA metrics, BERT Match (BEM) and
VQA score, averaged over the test set for evalua-
tion on E-VQA and Infoseek, respectively.
Models
We use the Qwen2-VL-Instruct (Wang
et al., 2024a) and Qwen3-VL-Instruct (Bai et al.,
2025) families of VLMs for answer generation. We
also apply BEFT to Llava-Llama-3 (XTuner, 2023)
on E-VQA. For retrieval, we use PreFLMR-L
(442M) and EVA-CLIP-8B following prior works.
BEFT Training
We provide K = 2 documents
for BEFT training on E-VQA, Infoseek, and MM-
Needle, where one document is the correct ground-
ing. We set K = 4 for SlideVQA and include the
prior loss (Eq. 7), so that all gold documents can
be included.
Fine-tuned Baselines
We establish Supervised
Fine-tuning (SFT) and Direct Preference Optimiza-
tion (DPO) baselines for comparison with BEFT.
Following prior work, we conduct SFT with the
Top-5 documents from PreFLMR-L retrieval in
context (Lin and Byrne, 2022). The Top-5 might
not contain the ground-truth document. For DPO,
we generate 8 samples per question using the SFT
model conditioned on the Top-5 documents, and
choose a correct response as preferred and an in-
correct response as dis-preferred to form pairwise
preference data following Yang et al. (2024). DPO
training is performed on the SFT model. We per-
form a grid-search to identify the optimal β value
for DPO. Full details are provided in Appendix F.

BEFT Performance
On the M2KR E-VQA set,

ence (Sec. 4.5). On Infoseek, BEFT achieves 45.1
on the M2KR test split and 45.4 on the full valida-
tion set, comparable to state-of-the-art systems.

4.5
Efficient BERAG Inference
In this section, we analyze the inference costs of
BERAG and study acceleration techniques.
Compute
We first analyze asymptotic compute
complexity for prefill and decode.
The prefill
time complexity is asymptotically O(KD2) for
BERAG and O(K2D2) for RAG given K doc-
uments with length D (Appendix J). This is be-
cause BERAG processes K length-D documents
in parallel, whereas standard RAG processes one
sequence of length K × D. For decoding, BERAG
requires the same asymptotic computation as RAG
O(K × D), assuming all KV caches are available.
However, in decoding BERAG pays an additional
cost of (K −1) × P to attend to the shared prompt
x (Eq. 2), where P is the length of x, and so we
expect BERAG decoding to be slower than RAG.
There are no savings in memory for BERAG, as
the KV caches for all documents still need to be
stored.
Memory
We now discuss memory usage in BE-
RAG inference. Let the vocabulary size be V . The
recursive form in Eq. 4 requires us to store the pre-
vious posterior (K floats) and the next-token like-
lihood at the previous step (V × K floats). Given
that V=150K for Qwen3-VL and assuming K=50,
this requires 45 MB of memory per BERAG re-
quest. In practice, we assign a buffer of 500 MB
on each GPU. This allows our inference engine
(vLLM-BERAG, see below) to handle ten K=50
user messages simultaneously. Once a message is
processed, this space can be freed to be used for
other requests.
Top-P Pruning Acceleration
We find a sim-
ple Top-P pruning procedure can substantially
speed up BERAG decoding: at each decoding
step, we keep the smallest number of documents
such that their cumulative document posterior mass
(Eq.3) exceeds P = 1 −
1
2K . This reduces com-
pute and memory requirements for decode, as the
KV cache associated with dropped documents no
longer needs to be stored and operated on. We
choose the threshold P = 1−1
2K as it naturally ad-
justs with respect to different K settings and does
not drop any document in the first pass given a
uniform prior.

These results show that BERAG document pos-
teriors can guide inference so that documents with
very small posterior weights can be safely dropped
without affecting answer generation. This is also an
indirect confirmation of the quality of the learned
document posterior.
Relative to concatenative
RAG, BERAG with Top-P pruning offers reduced
latency, improved decoding speed, along with BE-
RAG’s improved VQA performance.

6
Conclusion
We
introduce
Bayesian
Ensemble
Retrieval
Augmented Generation (BERAG), a Bayesian
ensemble-based RAG inference procedure along
with Bayesian Ensemble Fine-Tuning (BEFT) to
train BERAG systems end-to-end. We show BEFT-
trained BERAG systems give strong performance
on E-VQA and Infoseek by utilizing deep retrieval
lists. We also demonstrate BERAG mitigates the
“lost-in-the-middle” effect via position-invariant en-
sembles. Furthermore, we show BERAG is very
efficient in terms of inference. BERAG requires
shorter prefill time than standard RAG by design,
and can utilize document pruning to reduce decod-
ing time without performance degradation. We
show that BERAG achieves substantially faster in-
ference than standard RAG with long contexts in a
modern inference framework such as vLLM. Over-
all, BERAG is a simple, fast, and good-performing
approach to KB-VQA.
8

Limitations
We formulate BEFT as a likelihood-based fine-
tuning procedure. We have not explored reinforce-
ment learning based approaches. However, we
note that the BERAG framework provides a well-
formulated probability distribution over output se-
quences (Eq. 2) that could be incorporated into RL
for learning BERAG systems. In this work, we fo-
cus on direct optimization of the likelihood (BEFT)
and leave RL extensions to future work.
Another limitation is that we find that pre-trained
systems require BEFT training to perform useful
BERAG inference. This may limit BERAG to sce-
narios in which task-based fine-tuning is feasible,
although we note that BERAG training is effective
with lightweight methods such as LORA.

Extended Related Work
RAG with Document-level Ensembling
Aggre-
gating generations conditioned on individual doc-
uments is considered in early RAG works (Lewis
et al., 2020) and continues to receive academic at-
tention recently (Shi et al., 2024; Qiu et al., 2025;
Kim et al., 2026). RMCD (Kim et al., 2026) com-
bines logits from multiple retrieved contexts using
contrastive decoding, improving robustness to irrel-
evant contexts without additional training. Entropy-
based decoding (Qiu et al., 2025) proposes a similar
document-parallel ensemble decoding scheme but
uses entropy as heuristic signal to compute docu-
ment weights. Compared to these works, we derive
a principled probabilistic framework for inference
and show an end-to-end training technique for the
ensemble.

Tackling Lost-in-the-Middle Effect
Lost-in-the-
middle, a common failure mode of LLM-based
response generation arising from overlooking perti-
nent information positioned in the middle of LLM’s
context, has been studied in depth in recent liter-
ature (Liu et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2025; Laban
et al., 2026). The term was first coined by Liu
et al. (2024) and recently studied in multi-modal
contexts by Yao et al. (2025) and in multi-turn di-
alogue by Laban et al. (2026). These study report
substantial performance degradation from the lost-
in-the-middle and jointly identify the mitigation of
this effect as a critical topic for further research.
A wide range of mitigation approaches have
been proposed. Data-driven approaches attempt
to alleviate lost-in-the-middle by curating a train-
ing distribution that emphasize response genera-
tion based on the middle of the context (An et al.,
2024).
Another line of work focuses on long-
context friendly schemes of positional encoding:
CREAM (Wu et al., 2024) interpolates positional
encoding by manipulating position indices; Ms-
PoE (Zhang et al., 2024) introduces position-aware
head-wise re-scaling ratios to native RoPE (Su
et al., 2024) encoding; and V2PE (Ge et al., 2025)
proposes a separate encoding scheme for visual
tokens at variable and smaller increments. An-
other approach to tackle lost-in-the-middle is to
subscribe to a response pipeline that is designed to
handle long context. PAM QA (He et al., 2024) sub-
scribes to a fixed pipeline of Question Repetition,
Index Prediction, and finally Answer Summariza-
tion, whereas LongRAG (Zhao et al., 2024) intro-
duces a pipeline that includes CoT-guided filter-
ing and LLM-based information extractor to tackle
RAG tasks with long context.
Similar to previous works, we are also inter-
ested in establishing positional invariance. No-
tably, BERAG achieves this invariance without
13

modifying positional encoding or stipulating task-
specific response pipelines. To our knowledge, this
is the first work that addresses lost-in-the-middle
via positional-agnostic ensembles.

Experimental Setup Details
Training Details and Hyper-parameters
For
Infoseek, we subsample 60K examples from the
training set for SFT, DPO, and BEFT training. For
E-VQA, we use the full training set. We use a
LoRA rank of 64 in training with a learning rate of
1e-5.
Inference
We use FP16 weights and greedy de-
coding. We find that temperature sampling gives
similar performance and so have kept to determin-
istic decoding.
Libraries
We use LLaMAFactory (Zheng et al.,
2024) to perform supervised fine-tuning and DPO
training, and implement a custom trainer in LLa-
MAFactory for BEFT training. We use vLLM for
SFT and DPO inference, and implement BERAG
inference with the transformers (Wolf et al., 2020)
library and our custom vLLM-BERAG engine.
MLP head for document prior
We use a two-
layer MLP head s(·) to compute the document
prior (Eq.5). The MLP has the same hidden di-
mensions as the backbone vision language model.
For Qwen2-VL-Instruct, we use the ‘<im_end>‘
embedding. For LLaVA-LLama model, we use
the ‘<eot_id>‘ embedding. In training, we apply
a small learning rate of 1e-7 to fine-tune the MLP.
We find that using a larger learning rate (e.g., 1e-
5 as applied to the backbone VLM) will result in
unstable training and worse performance.
DPO β values
On E-VQA we use β = 0.7
and find that performance is insensitive to β ∈
(0.3, 0.9). On Infoseek, we find that a large value
of β = 1.5 is required to see gains from DPO.
This heavy regularization may be attributed to the
fact that Infoseek is curated based on very few
Wikipedia documents compared to its training set
size, which results in overfitting.

Learning to Predict Retrieval Success
It can be useful for the answer generation system
to predict whether the retrieval is successful, that
is, whether the ground-truth document is returned
in the Top-K list. We now show two decision rules
to predict retrieval success with BEFT systems:
(1) Prior-logits-based. We compute the relevance
probabilities of all individual documents following
the logistic classifier σ(si
k) in Eq.7. If any value ex-
ceeds 0.5, we flag a retrieval success. This decision
rule can be used directly by BEFT systems trained
according to Sec.4.1; (2) Posterior-based. We in-
clude an empty pseudo-passage z0. A retrieval
failure is flagged if the posterior (Eq. 3) assigns the
highest mass on z0. This decision rule is applied
to a variant of BEFT system, where in training we
replace the gold document with an empty pseudo-
passage z0 on 50% of the examples. We apply
the prior loss in training (Eq. 7) to encourage the
16

model to assign high posterior to z0 on retrieval
failure. We refer to system trained with this data as
BEFT[w/z0].

Asymptotic Analysis for BERAG
inference
In the analysis below we assume the standard,
quadratic attention mechanism for language gener-
ation. We note that BERAG also enjoys improve-
ment from any sub-quadratic version of attention
mechanism in that each branch in the ensemble
be computed more efficiently. We assume we are
given K documents each with length D.
Decode Analysis
In decoding, the incoming em-
bedding interacts with all previous tokens at every
layer, that is, the amount of compute required is de-
termined by the length of KV cache. For plain BE-
RAG, the length of KV cache is strictly larger than
that in standard RAG. This is because the BERAG
ensemble still need to store all document tokens
as KV cache. In addition, query tokens need to be
repeated K times for each branch in the ensemble.
The total KV cache is therefore strictly larger than
that in standard RAG due to the repeated query
tokens.
For BERAG with Top-P pruning, KV cache
associated with low-posterior documents can be
dropped, thereby leading to substantial speed-up in
decoding.
Prefill Analysis
The asymptotic compute com-
plexity for standard RAG prefill is O((KD)2) =
O(K2D2). This is because all documents are con-
catenated to form one context with length KD. For
BERAG prefill, the asymptotic compute complex-
17

ity is O(KD2), as each branch contains a length-D
context and there are K branches in total to cover
all documents. In this analysis, we have assumed
that the query length Q is negligible compared to
the document length D. We note that the query is
identical for every branch, and so a possible op-
timization is to encode the query once and reuse
the associated KV cache for every branch. We
leave such practical optimization techniques to fu-
ture work and focus on quantifying the asymptotic
behavior here.

Motivated by BERAG’s strong performance with

two established tasks with many items in context:

We also study inference optimization for BERAG

SoTA systems follow two

terpretable Evidence Selection.

rectly provided to the system for generation without
pre-processing and the document posterior (Eq. 3)
provides an ordering of slides as they contribute
to generation. Our BEFT model achieves an Ex-
act Match score of 90.4 and 69.6 on ES and QA,
representing a +15.4 and +6.4 gains compared to
recent baselines, respectively.
BERAG generalizes to multi-modal context with
multiple relevant documents.
SlideVQA con-
tains instances where multiple slides are labeled
as ground-truth. This is unlike E-VQA and Infos-
eek, which have a single ground-truth document.
The strong performance on SlideVQA shows that
BEFT can synthesize information from multiple
multi-modal documents in context. We provide
qualitative analysis in Appendix N.
