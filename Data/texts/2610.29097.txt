Multi-level Encoder with Global Topic for
Task-oriented Dialogue Summarization
Zhuoqi He, Peijie Huang*, Yuhong Xu, Youming Peng, Mingzhi Xu, Xinyang Lin
College of Mathematics and Informatics, South China Agricultural University, Guangzhou, China

Abstract—Task-oriented dialogue summarization aims to au-
tomatically extract key information to generate domain sum-
maries to improve service efficiency and quality. Task-oriented
dialogue is inherently logical and surrounds specific topic. How
to effectively capture the dialogue topic and the most salient
information becomes one of the major challenges of this task. In
this paper, we propose a task-oriented dialogue summarization
model utilizing a multi-level encoder with global topic (MLEGT).
This model discovers temporal and spatial dependencies through
a multi-level encoder, while simultaneously obtaining a simple yet
effective global topic in the process of graph construction. It not
only captures the crucial details but also breaks the long-range
limit and discover the intrinsic structure across the dialogue.
Then, a global topic guided pointer mechanism is added into
the summrizer to capture ignored but important information,
which is helpful to improve the accuracy of the summary. A
comprehensive study of two public datasets, including a real-
world large-scale Chinese police interrogation dataset and a
medical reporting service dataset proves the superiority of our
method on several strong baselines.

I. INTRODUCTION
In an active industry service system, massive domain-
specific task-oriented spoken dialogues conveying important
information between human and human/agent are generated
in real-time. Automatically summarizing such task-oriented
dialogues may aid the archiving, indexing and retrieval of var-
ious records of oral communication such as sales interactions,
medical diagnosis, complain report to improve the service.
Therefore, how to transform a large number of task-oriented
dialogues into natural, concise, and core-informed texts such
as task-oriented dialogue summaries is gaining significantly
increased attention [1]–[3].
Most of the existing dialogue summary works are mainly
focused on open-domain dialogues, like chitchat [4]–[7], meet-
ing [8]–[10], which are usually summarized by stringing all
dialogue points to maintain an integral conversation flow [11].
Nevertheless, in task-oriented dialogues, dialogue speakers
commonly have strong and clear motivations and aim to
address issues about a specific task. To this end, recent
works focus on developing topic or structure-aware models for
task-oriented dialogue summarization. Zou et al. [1] leverage
topic information that flows in the dialogue to help generate

topic-aware summaries. Liu et al. [12] introduce a key point
sequence as auxiliary label, which helps the model learn the
logic of the summary. Zhao et al. [2] propose a dialogue
state-aware summarization model to combine original dialogue
text and structured dialogue state. However, these approaches
remain at a coarse level in exploring the intrinsic structural
features of dialogue. Gao et al. [3] propose a graph-based
dialogue summarization model that uses structure fusion graph
to capture the dialogue structure. However, this method is
lacking in topic modeling.
To address the above issues, we design a graph-based task-
oriented summarization model using multi-level encoder with
global topic (MLEGT). A multi-level encoder is proposed
to discover the temporal and spatial dependencies across the
dialogue. Simultaneously a simple yet effective global topic
is obtained in the process of graph construction, which helps
us grasp the flow of information and discover the intrinsic
structure across the dialogue. Then, we incorporate a global
topic guided pointer mechanism into the summrizer to cap-
ture the ignored but important information while generating
summary, which reduce the errors for better summarization.

B. Graph Encoder
1) Graph Construction: In this subsection, following Gao
et al. [3], we construct two types of graphs based on keywords
and speakers, which is the Keywords Graph and the Speaker
Graph respectively. We propose a new method in graph
construction for effectively ensuring the connectivity of the
graph. Our method is significantly different from Gao et al.
[3] in the following two points: (1) We set the diagonal of
the adjacency matrix be 1 so that each utterance can not only
discover the relations among their neighbors but also focus on
themselves. (2) We add a global node into each graph, which
can help us obtain a simple but effective global topic in the
process of graph construction.
Keywords Graph. The construction of Keywords Graph
aims to discover the spatial dependencies among different
utterances in the dialogue. Firstly, we obtain an utterances-
keywords adjacent matrix A based on keywords extracted from
utterances by SpaCy. Each value Ai,j in the adjacent matrix
corresponds the frequency of the j-th keyword occurrences in
i-th utterance. Let A multiply by its own transpose matrix,
and then we obtain the initial Keywords Graph Gk,

Global Node. Furthermore, we incorporate a global node
into each graph, which is a hyper node [14] connected to all
nodes, to aggregate non-local information across the dialogue.
Each node builds relationships with its neighboring nodes
through edges, while a global node connects to all nodes,
serving as a central hub for aggregating global information.
It can interact with all nodes and capture important features
in the process of information propagation, thereby enhancing
the model’s overall understanding and modeling capability of
the graph structure.

D. Training Objective
We optimize all parameters of MLEGT by employing the
cross-entropy loss between the generated summary and the
ground truth summary as the training objective. And we
initialize the relevant parameters in our Transformer-based
utterance encoder and summary generator with the parameters
from the pre-trained language model BART.
III. EXPERIMENTS
A. Experimental Setup
1) Datasets: The datasets used in this experiment are LC-
SPIRT1 [19] and Medical DS2 [20]. LCSPIRT is a large-scale
Chinese text summarization corpus containing 38,500 text-
summary pairs derived from police inquiry dialogue records,
and the texts are deidentified to ensure privacy. Medical DS,
on the other hand, consists of 3,260 dialogue-summary pairs
collected from the pediatric department of a Chinese online
healthcare community. For both datasets, we use the originally
proposed train, validation, and test splits, comprising 70%,
10%, and 20% of the data, respectively.
2) Implementation Details: We use BART as the token-
level encoder, and the outputs are utilized into the follow
utterance-level encoder as well. We apply Adam with a
learning rate 1e-5 for BART and the training epoch is set
to 15. All Transformer blocks have 4 layers, 8 heads, 1024
hidden units. All hyper-parameters tuning is conducted on the
validation set.
3) Baselines: We compare MLEGT with following base-
lines: PGN [18], BART [13], T5-pegasus [21], TDS+NTM
[1], GPT-4o (few-shot) [22] and SDDS [3]. We use the SDDS
without the discourse parsing toolkit for fair comparison.

B. Experiments Results
1) Main Results: We assessed the quality of summarization
using Rouge F1 score, including Rouge-1, Rouge-2, and
Rouge-L metrics [23]. Table I presents the results on the test
set for various models. We highlight best and second best
results. Notably, our model surpasses all compared base-
lines, achieving state-of-the-art performance across all metrics.
Specifically, on the LCSPIRT dataset, our model exhibits
significant improvements, up by +3.5% in ROUGE-1, +3.48%
in ROUGE-2, and +4.21% in ROUGE-L scores. We also find
that our MLEGT can achieve consistently better performance
than the strong baselines on Medical DS. To our surprise, PGN
that performs poorly on LCSPIRT gets the best ROUGE-2
score on Medical DS. We conjecture the main reason is that
there are numerous medical terms scattered throughout the
long conversation structure in the medical dataset, and the
pointer mechanism is good at capturing such information while
generating summary.
2) Ablation Study: We further investigate the effect of each
mechanism of MLEGT by removing the global node and the
pointer mechanism, respectively. The results are also shown in
Table I. The results of “w/o global topic” and “w/o pointer”
demonstrate a notable decline on both LCSPIRT and Medical
DS datasets. This decline underscores the critical role of the
proposed global topic as well as the global topic guided pointer
mechanism.
3) Human Evaluation: We conducted human evaluation
to qualitatively evaluate the generated summaries. As our
research focuses on task-oriented dialogue summarization,
the informativeness is more critical than the conciseness
and fluency for the generated summaries. We focus on how
well the generated summaries covered the most important
content and compare the accuracy of entity generation across
different methods. We randomly selected 200 samples from
the LCSPIRT test set and asked five human volunteers to
manually annotate entity information such as the Start and End
Year/Month/Day/Time, Character, and Place. Our volunteers
then compared the entities in the generated summaries of
MLEGT and SDDS with those in the reference summaries to
count the correct rate of entity generation. As illustrated in Fig.
3, MLEGT demonstrates better quality in entity recognition,
particularly for End Month, End Date, End Time, Character,
and Place. It demonstrates that MLEGT can identify the more

IV. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose a model that utilizes a multi-level
encoder with global topic (MLEGT) for task-oriented dialogue
summarization, which enables to discover the temporal and
spatial dependencies while obtain a simple yet effective global
topic to better capture the crucial information. Experiments
on two public data sets demonstrate the effectiveness of our
proposed model in quantitative and qualitative evaluation.
