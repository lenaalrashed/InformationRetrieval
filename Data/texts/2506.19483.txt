Abstract This paper provides preliminary results on exploring the task of perform-
ing turn-level data augmentation for dialogue system based on different types of
commonsense relationships, and the automatic evaluation of the generated synthetic
turns. The proposed methodology takes advantage of the extended knowledge and
zero-shot capabilities of pretrained Large Language Models (LLMs) to follow in-
structions, understand contextual information, and their commonsense reasoning
capabilities. The approach draws inspiration from methodologies like Chain-of-
Thought (CoT), applied more explicitly to the task of prompt-based generation for
dialogue-based data augmentation conditioned on commonsense attributes, and the
automatic evaluation of the generated dialogues.
To assess the effectiveness of the proposed approach, first we extracted 200 ran-
domly selected partial dialogues, from 5 different well-known dialogue datasets,
and generate alternative responses conditioned on different event commonsense at-
tributes. This novel dataset allows us to measure the proficiency of LLMs in gen-
erating contextually relevant commonsense knowledge, particularly up to 12 dif-
ferent specific ATOMIC [10] database relations. Secondly, we propose an evalua-
tion framework to automatically detect the quality of the generated dataset inspired

1 Introduction
The challenge of instilling commonsense intelligence in AI systems, especially in
Natural Language Processing (NLP), represents a significant yet challenging goal.
Human-like reasoning about everyday situations and events remains a complex task
for AI systems [25]. In this context, Large Language Models (LLMs), such as Chat-
GPT [20] and GPT-4 [21], have emerged as pivotal enablers in this domain [16].
Their robust zero-shot and few-shot learning capabilities have demonstrated sub-
stantial improvements across various NLP tasks [13]. Due to their general-purpose
design, they are easy to adapt to downstream tasks, endowing them with the neces-
sary background knowledge of the physical and social world. Beyond the conven-
tional approach of model fine-tuning where all model parameters are adjusted [8],
in-context learning methods have been shown to achieve high performance in mod-
ulating LLM’s behavior through prompts [18].
AI’s commonsense reasoning has evolved from the structured logic [30] [10] to
more context-rich approaches [6] [5]. Former approaches primarily structure com-
monsense knowledge in logic-based formalisms, while the latter approaches empha-
size in natural language-based inferences for dialogue reasoning. Aproaches such
as COMET [2] focuses on generating novel commonsense knowledge by adapting
pretrained neural language models to create new knowledge tuples. Likewise, our
preliminary research pivots around the concept of redefining commonsense knowl-
edge representation by developing a dataset using a prompt-based approach. Moving
away from conventional knowledge graph and logical expression methodologies, we
propose to harness the expansive potential of LLMs. Using the relational framework
of the ATOMIC[10] dataset as a grounding, our goal is to generate new dialog turns
in natural language. These turns are designed to be instilled with commonsense
knowledge that is contextually relevant and nuanced, reflecting commonsense rela-
tional insights. This approach not only promises a more human-like interaction in
AI-driven dialogues, but also aligns with the natural progression of AI towards a
more intuitive understanding of human communication [4].

2 Background
In recent years, the goal of making machines manifest commonsense understanding
and capabilities like humans has been an active field for conversational AI. Although
early research looked at how machines understand basic facts and ideas [19] [14] [15]
and [17], a new approach has emerged by means of LLMs. Building upon the avail-
able Commonsense Knowledge Bases (CSKB), LLMs have enabled to foster less
abstract forms of representing social commonsense knowledge. ConceptNet [30]
and ATOMIC [10] have emerged as widely used resources for dataset curation, es-
tablishing a de facto standard.
Our exploratory research takes ATOMIC as foundation and examines two in-
tersectional areas in conversational AI: the synthesis of commonsense datasets via
prompt-based processing, and the automatic quality assessment of the generated
commonsense content. This dual focus addresses the critical need for not only gen-
erating contextually rich commonsense knowledge, but also ensuring its alignment
with the intended parameters of conversational AI systems. Our work aims to refine
and advance these mutually linked aspects, thus contributing to the development of
more nuanced and reliable conversational agents.
Commonsense Dataset Synthesis Various works has already addressed the is-
sue of curating commonsense reasoning from foundational commonsense symbolic
datasets. Ghosal [6] presented CIDER, a dataset marked by commonsense infer-
ences in dialogues. This work was further developed with CICERO [5] in 2022. CI-
CERO expanded the event-based reasoning annotations for better commonsense in-
ference in dialogue systems. Different researchers have delved into particular kinds
of commonsense such as exploring temporal aspects [22] or examining ethical di-
mensions [36] [11] [7].

Beyond the existing methodologies based on using and filtering of pre-existed
datasets, SODA [12] opened a new stream of research. Its approach aims at dis-
tilling new generalist dialogues from the implicit commonsense knowledge that is
already embedded in LLMs. This synthesis of dialogues seeks to overcome the ex-
isting limitations in generation, evaluation and understanding due to the dependence
on these small datasets. Along these lines, SYNDICOM [24] further pushes these
ideas proposing a methodology based on the incorporation of valid and invalid an-
swers with feedback to dialogue contexts. Its methodology aims to improve the
predictive capacity of the system’s dialogues. These responses are obtained from
templates created with the logical formalisms contained in ATOMIC and chaining
these inferences together to form multiple dialogue turns.
Different to previous research, we propose a synthesis methodology at turn-level.
Relying on the use of several open-domain databases, we expand the implicit knowl-
edge of the following turn in a contextualized dialogue by considering up to 12 dif-
ferent commonsense dimensions. The advantage is that we are able to provide new
alternative turn responses that focuses on a particular commonsense aspect.
Commonsense Evaluation with LLMs Thanks to the wide general-purpose ca-
pabilities that LLMs provide, their use have been spread to all research layers. The
strategies followed to evaluate vary in both procedure and application. This paper
focuses on exploring their performance as zero-shot classification systems, where
no task-specific supervised training is provided to the model. Our proposal builds
on a number of recent works [29] [31] [23] [28] that directly use LLMs as clas-
sifiers in a multi-stage classification process, where the main focus is on prompt-
engineering to perform the classification task. Although there is much effort towards
open-sourcing competitive LLMs, OpenAI’s ChatGPT and GPT-4 remain the most
successful LLMs to date [32], and therefore we used them in our experiments.
Similar to our approach, SODA [12] uses GPT-3.5 to automatically validate the
type of commonsense found in conversations that had previously been generated
from one of ATOMIC’s commonsense attributes. ACCENT [26] explicitly focuses
on computing continuous commonsense alignment scores by relying on the use of an
event extraction model and a generative commonsense model. Taking these works
as a starting point, our idea follows these same lines of commonsense contradiction
detection, but extending the turn-level commonsense dimensions to develop fine-
grained detection. Finally, we also include the usage of ACCENT as a means to
compare the results obtained with LLMs.
3 Methods
Inspired by recent prompt-based commonsense synthesis methods, we describe a
method to expand and evaluate commonsense at turn-level for dialogue systems.

Commonsense Synthesis using Large Language Models
5
3.1 Commonsense Knowledge Graph
Our idea is to expand the turns of given dialogues along certain given commonsense
knowledge dimensions. For this research, we selected ATOMIC as the reference
CSKB. It includes knowledge about physical entities, events-centered and social
interactions. Its components focused on events and social interactions correspond to
84.4% over the entire CSKB and provide information on how human events/actions
interact with other events/actions. We considered these relationships as a reference
for the definition of our dimensions that we want to expand in each turn. Table 1
shows our definitions for each type of symbolic relation taken from ATOMIC.
3.2 Source and Test Data
To build our experimental dataset we use dyadic dialogues from five datasets: Dai-
lyDialog [38], Topical-Chat [41], EmpatheticDialogues [37], Persona-Chat [40, 39]
and Wizard Of Wikipedia [42].
For each dataset, 40 random dialogues with 5 to 10 turns were selected. These
dialogues were expanded using the GPT-3.5 model; for the GPT-4 model, only 20
of the 40 selected dialogues were used. The decision to choose a lower number of
dialogs to be expanded with GPT-4 is primarily for economic reasons, given the
preliminary nature of this paper. Moreover, it is also assumed that, in general, GPT-
4 performs better than GPT-3. Under these assumptions, we decided to halve the
number of dialogs expanded with GPT-4. After this selection process, 1,721 turns
were augmented a total of 12 times each turn, once for each dimension in Table 1.
The augmented responses obtained by the LLM were on average 35% longer than
the original sentence, showing that the new responses incorporate more information
than the original response (probably due to the incorporation of commonsense in-
formation, but also a typical characteristic of the LLMs in spite of being requested
to be concise in the prompt instruction).
3.3 Methods and Prompt Design
Recent work has shown that zero-shot listwise prompt-based give better results for
classifying several text fragments simultaneously [28] [23]. On this basis, we pose
that, despite the fact that these statements have been claimed to classify natural
language text, similar results can be obtained for data synthesis by following an
equivalent approach. In our case, the synthesis of the responses at turn-level by
inducing given definitions of each knowledge commonsense dimension.
Therefore, our problem is stated: given a set of commonsense attribute definitions
R = {r1,...,rM}, a dialogue context D = {ti−n,...,ti−1,ti} and the task description p,
the job of the language model is to return a list of responses X = {x1,...,xM} at turn

The expansion process consists of generating new turns of dialogues following
the commonsense given by the definitions of ATOMIC relations.
Although this paper explores zero-shot generation, we have also performed one-
shot tests with examples generated automatically for each turn using COMET. To
do so, we concatenate a specific example for each definition, replacing the example
label shown in Table 1. The rationale behind this is to evaluate the performance
when the system is induced with knowledge that could potentially be integrated
versus self-generated knowledge.
On the other hand, the evaluation process is defined as a listwise prompt-based
classification process. Taking as ground-truths each relation that identifies the ex-
pansion of each turn, the GPT models are proposed to sort the definitions of the
relations in the form of an ordered list. Thus, the task is to identify the definitions
that best fit the expanded response. Once ranked, recommender system metrics are
taken to evaluate the performance of the system for the evaluation task.

5 Conclusions and Future Work
In this study, we introduce a listwise zero-shot generation and reranking approach
powered by open-source large language models. Our initial experiments show the
viability of using LLMs for automatic generation and evaluation of commonsense

dialogue turns. Both GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 generations were found very good (being
GPT-3.5 a suitable option in case of considering costs), while for evaluation GPT-4
performed the best. In future work, we plan to increase the number of dialogues
and perform manual annotations to better detect which commonsense attributes are
better reflected in the expanded turns. Finally, we also plan to fine-tune a small
generative model that can be used to automatically extract the attributes in chitchat
dialogues.
